Common Property Concerns by Loan Type

Some home inspection findings may overlap with conditions reviewed by lenders, appraisers, or insurance companies. Find your loan type below to see common examples—not a list of automatic repair requirements.

Conventional Loans

Generally more flexible with minor defects and maintenance.

  • Major roof damage or active leaks
  • Significant foundation or structural defects
  • Unsafe electrical wiring or panels
  • Missing or defective GFCI protection where hazardous
  • Major plumbing leaks or water intrusion
  • Nonfunctional heating
  • Unsafe stairs, porches, or decks

FHA Loans

Emphasizes safety, security, and structural soundness.

  • Peeling paint on pre-1978 homes
  • Leaking or significantly deteriorated roofs
  • Exposed wiring or other electrical hazards
  • Missing or defective GFCI protection where hazardous
  • Unsafe stairs, railings, or broken glass
  • Nonfunctional heating, plumbing, or sanitation
  • Significant foundation or moisture problems
  • Wood-destroying insect damage

VA Loans

Focuses on safe, sanitary, structurally sound housing.

  • Active roof leaks or major deterioration
  • Peeling paint on pre-1978 homes
  • Unsafe wiring or GFCI-related shock hazards
  • Unsafe stairs, railings, or decks
  • Significant structural damage
  • Inadequate heating, water, or sanitation
  • Serious water intrusion or drainage issues
  • Wood-destroying insect activity or damage

USDA Loans

Generally shares many safety and condition concerns with FHA.

  • Significant roof or structural defects
  • Peeling paint on pre-1978 homes
  • Electrical hazards or hazardous missing GFCI protection
  • Unsafe stairs or railings
  • Nonfunctional heating or plumbing
  • Significant water intrusion
  • Inadequate water or sewage systems
  • Wood-destroying insect damage

Homeowners Insurance

Insurers may have separate requirements regardless of financing type.

  • Aging, damaged, or deteriorated roofs
  • Unsafe wiring or certain outdated electrical panels
  • Missing or defective GFCI protection where hazardous
  • Plumbing leaks or certain older piping materials
  • Missing smoke alarms
  • Unsafe stairs, decks, or railings
  • Overhanging trees or exterior hazards
  • Significant property deterioration

Important: Inspection Findings Are Not Loan Requirements

Home inspection reports do not identify or label items as loan-, appraisal-, or insurance-related requirements, and inspectors do not determine loan or insurance eligibility. These examples are not automatic repair requirements. Review your findings with your real estate agent, lender, and insurance provider to determine whether any conditions apply and whether addressing them proactively is appropriate.
